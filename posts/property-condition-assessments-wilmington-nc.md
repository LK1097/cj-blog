A property condition assessment (PCA) in Wilmington, NC is a walk-through survey and document review of a commercial property that identifies physical deficiencies, immediate repairs and expected capital needs. On the coast, the assessment should look hard at the things salt air, humidity and hurricane season do to buildings: corroded metals, roof attachment, drainage and past water intrusion.

## What does a commercial PCA cover in Wilmington?

A commercial PCA covers the major building systems and the site, and turns what the assessor observes into a prioritized list of repairs and a forecast of capital needs. Most Wilmington PCAs are written to [ASTM E2018](https://store.astm.org/e2018-24.html), the industry's baseline guide for property condition assessments.

A typical scope includes:

- **Roofing:** covering, flashing, edge metal, drainage and the attachment of rooftop equipment
- **Structure and envelope:** foundations, framing, exterior walls, sealants, windows, doors and balconies
- **Mechanical, electrical and plumbing:** HVAC equipment age and condition, electrical service and distribution, domestic water and sanitary systems
- **Life safety and fire safety:** fire alarm and sprinkler observations, egress, emergency lighting and fire extinguisher service tags
- **Accessibility:** a visual survey of common accessibility features
- **Site:** paving, parking, curbs, sidewalks, stormwater drainage, retaining walls and site lighting
- **Documents and interviews:** available drawings, permits, service records, prior reports and conversations with property management

The deliverable is a report with photographs, an immediate repair list and a capital reserve table for the expected hold or loan term. The exact scope depends on your assignment, so agree the standard, the exclusions and the deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.

## Which coastal conditions should a Wilmington PCA look for?

Coastal exposure is the single biggest difference between a Wilmington PCA and one in the Piedmont. Ask your assessor to address these conditions specifically.

### Salt air and corrosion

Salt-laden air speeds up corrosion on exposed metal. On commercial properties this shows up on rooftop HVAC condensers and cabinets, roof edge metal and fasteners, exterior stair stringers and railings, balcony connections, light poles and storefront frames. Corrosion shortens the useful life of equipment and components, so it changes the capital reserve schedule, not just the repair list.

### Wind and roof attachment

Wilmington sits in hurricane country. Hurricane Florence made landfall near Wrightsville Beach in September 2018, and its flooding cut the city off by road for days. Many roofs in the area have been repaired or replaced since then, and the quality of that work varies.

A useful PCA looks at roof covering condition, perimeter securement, flashing and the tie-downs on rooftop units. Ask the assessor to note evidence of past storm repairs and to record roof age and any available warranty information.

### Water intrusion, humidity and drainage

Heavy rain and high humidity put extra load on building envelopes and HVAC systems. Look for staining at ceilings and window heads, failed sealant joints, efflorescence on masonry and signs of mold-friendly conditions in interior spaces. On the site side, poorly graded paving, clogged catch basins and ponding near foundations are common findings on low-lying coastal parcels.

### Flood exposure

A PCA records physical evidence such as high-water staining, grading and drainage performance. It does not determine flood zone status. Confirm the flood designation through the [FEMA Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal) and your lender's requirements, and treat flood insurance and elevation questions as a separate workstream.

## Which property types need a PCA in Wilmington?

Any commercial asset being bought, refinanced or held for capital planning can justify a PCA. In Wilmington, the current pipeline makes a few property types especially common.

- **Industrial and flex:** New flex and small-bay buildings are going up in northern New Hanover County, Leland and along U.S. 421. Newer buildings still need a baseline PCA to document warranties, punch-list items and early drainage issues. Older warehouse stock near the port needs closer attention to roofs, dock equipment and paving.
- **Retail centers:** With vacancy this low, strip centers and neighborhood retail trade quickly. Roofs, parking lots and rooftop HVAC usually drive the capital budget.
- **Medical and small office:** Demand for medical office around the Novant Health New Hanover Regional Medical Center area, Mayfaire, Ogden and Porters Neck is pushing conversions of older office and retail space. Conversions raise questions about mechanical capacity, plumbing and accessibility.
- **Multifamily:** Garden-style communities need close review of exterior stairs, balconies, breezeways, siding and unit HVAC, all of which are exposed to coastal weather.
- **Hospitality:** Hotels near the beaches and downtown face heavy wear on HVAC equipment, finishes and exterior components.

## How do lenders use a PCA on Wilmington deals?

Lenders use the PCA to size required repairs and capital reserves and to confirm that the collateral is in the condition the borrower describes. Many commercial lenders, along with agency, CMBS and SBA programs, set their own report requirements on top of ASTM E2018. These can include a specific reserve term, cost tables, inflation assumptions or a particular report format.

Get the lender's written requirements before you request a proposal. A PCA that does not match the lender's template can delay closing while the report is reissued.

## How do you order a PCA in Wilmington?

Ordering a PCA is straightforward when you send complete information up front.

1. **Define the decision.** State whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or internal capital planning.
2. **Collect lender requirements.** Get the lender's written report requirements and reserve term.

## What does a PCA not replace?

A PCA reports physical conditions that can be observed during the site visit. It does not replace a Phase I environmental site assessment, a zoning report, title review, lease review, a survey or legal advice. It is not a code compliance inspection. Concealed conditions and systems that were not tested may need follow-up by a structural engineer, roofer or other specialist. Permit history and code questions should be confirmed with the [City of Wilmington](https://www.wilmingtonnc.gov/Development-Business/Zoning/Building-Permits) or New Hanover County.
