<h2>What a commercial PCA covers in Richmond</h2>
<p>A PCA is a documented walk-through and records review of a commercial property, usually written to the ASTM E2018 standard. For older mixed-use and office buildings, retail centers, multifamily, and industrial and warehouse space, the report typically covers:</p>
<ul>
<li>Roof and roof drainage</li>
<li>Structure, foundation and building envelope</li>
<li>HVAC, electrical and plumbing systems</li>
<li>Life-safety and accessibility observations</li>
<li>Site, paving, drainage and parking areas</li>
<li>Immediate repairs and a schedule of expected capital needs</li>
</ul>
<p>The exact scope depends on your assignment. Agree the standard, exclusions and deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.</p>
<h2>What to ask about in central Virginia</h2>
<p>Richmond has an older building stock than most Carolinas markets, including historic and converted buildings. Older masonry, original windows, and aging mechanical and electrical systems are common items to price into capital planning.</p>
<p>Virginia winters mean freeze-thaw cycles. Ask the assessor to look closely at masonry, parapets, roof membranes and pavement, where moisture and temperature swings do the most damage.</p>
<p>For properties in historic districts or with older additions, confirm early whether any planned repairs could face review requirements. That is a question for local authorities and counsel, not something a PCA settles.</p>
<h2>How to order a PCA in Richmond</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Define the decision.</strong> Say whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or an internal capital plan.</li>
<li><strong>Collect lender requirements.</strong> Get the lender's written report requirements before you request a proposal.</li>
<li><strong>Send property details.</strong> Provide the address, property type, size, year built, known issues and your deadline.</li>
<li><strong>Arrange access.</strong> Ask the seller or property manager to confirm roof, equipment room and tenant space access before the site visit.</li>
<li><strong>Review the draft.</strong> Trace each major finding back to its photos and observations, then assign an owner to every open item.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What a PCA does not replace</h2>
<p>A PCA reports on physical conditions that can be observed. It does not replace an environmental site assessment, a zoning report, a title review, lease review or legal advice. Concealed defects and untested systems may need specialist follow-up, and local code, permitting and flood-zone questions should be confirmed with the relevant authorities.</p>
