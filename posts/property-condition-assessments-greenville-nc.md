A property condition assessment (PCA) in Greenville, NC is a walk-through survey and document review of a commercial property that identifies physical deficiencies, immediate repairs and expected capital needs. In Greenville, the most important questions are usually flood history near the Tar River, wear in student-oriented apartments around East Carolina University, and the age of roofs, HVAC and paving in the city's medical office, retail and light industrial buildings.

## Why this matters for Greenville buyers and lenders

Greenville is the commercial center of eastern North Carolina, and two anchors shape most of its income property: a large public university and a regional academic medical center. Both create steady demand, and both produce building types with specific capital risks.

East Carolina University counted [27,153 students for the fall 2025 semester](https://news.ecu.edu/2025/09/10/enrollment-up-at-ecu-for-third-consecutive-year/), its third straight year of enrollment growth. That base supports a large stock of purpose-built student housing and older apartments rented by the bed, where turnover is high and interiors wear faster than in conventional multifamily. Medical and professional office buildings cluster around the health sciences campus and the hospital district, and retail and flex buildings line the city's main commercial corridors.

The other constant is water. When Hurricane Floyd stalled over eastern North Carolina in September 1999, the Tar River at Greenville peaked at about 29.7 feet, according to the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://pubs.usgs.gov/wri/wri004093/flooding.html), far above anything in the short gauge record before it. ECU's library notes that the flood damaged residence halls and academic buildings near the river and forced the evacuation of several thousand students and staff. Buildings that took water in 1999, or in later storms, may carry repairs and hidden damage that a buyer needs to understand before closing.

## What does a commercial PCA cover in Greenville?

A commercial PCA covers the major building systems and the site, and turns what the assessor observes into a prioritized list of repairs and a forecast of capital needs. Most PCAs for lenders and institutional buyers are written to [ASTM E2018](https://store.astm.org/e2018-24.html), the industry's baseline guide for property condition assessments.

A typical scope includes:

- **Roofing:** covering, flashing, parapets, drainage and the attachment of rooftop equipment
- **Structure and envelope:** foundations, framing, masonry, exterior walls, sealants, windows, doors and balconies
- **Mechanical, electrical and plumbing:** HVAC equipment age and condition, electrical service and distribution, domestic water and sanitary systems
- **Life safety and fire safety:** fire alarm and sprinkler observations, egress, emergency lighting and fire extinguisher service tags
- **Accessibility:** a visual survey of common accessibility features
- **Site:** paving, parking, curbs, sidewalks, stormwater drainage, retaining walls and site lighting
- **Documents and interviews:** available drawings, permits, service records, prior reports and conversations with property management

The deliverable is a report with photographs, an immediate repair list and a capital reserve table for the expected hold or loan term. Agree the standard, the exclusions and the deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.

## How should a PCA handle flood risk in Greenville?

A PCA should record visible evidence of past flooding and the features that limit future water damage, then point the buyer to the separate reports that answer the regulatory questions. The condition survey and the flood zone determination are different deliverables, and lenders usually require both.

### Signs of past flood damage

Look for water lines on walls and equipment, replaced drywall or flooring at a consistent height, rusted door frames and electrical boxes near the floor, efflorescence on lower masonry, and mismatched materials that suggest a partial rebuild. Ask the seller and property manager directly about past flood events and insurance claims, and request the related repair records.

### Equipment elevation

Note where HVAC condensers, electrical panels, switchgear, water heaters and elevator equipment sit relative to grade. Equipment on the ground floor or in basements near the river is exposed to the next flood, and relocating it can be a large capital item.

### Site drainage

Eastern North Carolina is flat and its soils often drain slowly. Record ponding on paving, clogged inlets, failed downspout connections, eroded swales and slopes that send water toward the building. Short heavy rains can cause trouble even on properties far from the river.

### What the PCA does not decide

A PCA does not determine a property's flood zone, its base flood elevation or whether past repairs met floodplain rules. Get a flood zone determination through your lender, check FEMA flood maps, and confirm local floodplain development requirements with the City of Greenville or Pitt County before you budget elevation or floodproofing work.

## What should a PCA look for in Greenville student housing?

In student housing, the capital budget usually turns on interior wear, unit HVAC, plumbing and exterior wood components. Units leased by the bed turn over every year, and the same finishes and equipment take far more abuse than in a conventional apartment.

- **Unit interiors:** Sample enough units to see the real range of condition. Note flooring, cabinets, countertops, doors, hardware and appliances, and record when the last full renovation took place.
- **Unit HVAC and water heaters:** Record equipment ages from nameplates. Staggered replacement across hundreds of units is often the largest recurring reserve item.
- **Plumbing:** Look for leaks, water staining below bathrooms and evidence of drain line problems, which multiply when four bedrooms share a unit.
- **Stairs, balconies and breezeways:** Wood stairs and balconies on 1980s through 2000s garden communities deteriorate under coastal plain humidity. Look for rot, loose railings, corroded connectors and failed flashing at ledger boards.
- **Life safety:** Check fire extinguisher tags, smoke alarm presence in sampled units, exit signage, emergency lighting and fire separation where visible, since occupancy is dense.
- **Amenities:** Pools, clubhouses, fitness rooms and gated access systems add capital items that conventional apartments may not have.

## What should a PCA look for in Greenville medical office and retail buildings?

For medical office, the capital budget usually turns on HVAC capacity, plumbing, roofs and accessibility. For retail centers, it turns on roofs, paving and storefront systems.

- **Medical office HVAC:** Exam rooms, imaging suites and procedure spaces carry heavier mechanical loads than standard offices. Record rooftop unit and air handler ages, and note supplemental cooling added by tenants.
- **Plumbing:** Medical suites have many fixtures and sinks. Look for leaks, corrosion and water heater age.
- **Accessibility:** Patients include people with limited mobility, so record visible accessibility features at parking, entrances, restrooms and routes between buildings. A PCA survey of accessibility is not a full accessibility compliance audit.
- **Retail roofs and paving:** Strip centers along the main commercial corridors often have large low-slope roofs and parking lots near the end of their useful life. Record roof type, age, ponding and patching, and note cracking, potholes and drainage failures in the lot.
- **Storefronts and signage:** Note failed sealants, damaged glazing, corroded frames and sign attachments.

Tenant-specific medical equipment, such as imaging machines or medical gas systems, is outside a standard PCA. Add a specialist review if those systems matter to your underwriting.

## How do lenders use a PCA on Greenville deals?

Lenders use the PCA to size required repairs and capital reserves and to confirm that the collateral is in the condition the borrower describes. Many commercial lenders, along with agency, CMBS and SBA programs, set their own report requirements on top of ASTM E2018, such as a specific reserve term, cost tables or a particular report format.

Get the lender's written requirements before you request a proposal. A PCA that does not match the lender's template can delay closing while the report is reissued. ## How do you order a PCA in Greenville?

Ordering a PCA goes fastest when you send complete information up front.

1. **Define the decision.** State whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or internal capital planning.
2. **Collect lender requirements.** Get the lender's written report requirements and reserve term.
3. **Send property details.** Provide the address, property type, building size, year built, unit or bed count, the number of buildings, known issues, any flood history and your deadline.
4. **Share documents.** Send available drawings, permits, roof warranties, service contracts, flood repair records and prior reports.
5. **Arrange access.** Ask the seller or property manager to confirm roof, mechanical room, vacant unit and occupied unit access before the site visit. In student housing, schedule around move-in and exam periods where you can.
6. **Review the draft.** Trace every major finding to its photographs and assign an owner to each open item before closing.

## What does a PCA not replace?

A PCA reports physical conditions that can be observed during the site visit. It does not replace a Phase I environmental site assessment, a flood zone determination, a zoning report, title review, lease review, a survey or legal advice. It is not a code compliance inspection. Permit history and code questions should be confirmed with the [City of Greenville Inspections Division](https://greenvillenc.gov/government/planning-and-development-services/inspections-division), which enforces the North Carolina State Building Codes inside the city and its extraterritorial jurisdiction, or with the Pitt County Permitting Center for unincorporated areas. Concealed conditions and systems that were not tested may need follow-up by a structural engineer, roofer, mechanical contractor or other specialist.
