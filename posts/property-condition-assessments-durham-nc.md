A property condition assessment (PCA) in Durham, NC is a walk-through survey and document review of a commercial property that identifies physical deficiencies, immediate repairs and expected capital needs. Durham's building stock is unusually mixed: century-old tobacco warehouses converted to offices and apartments, lab and flex buildings around Research Triangle Park, and a large inventory of suburban office and garden apartments, and each carries different capital risk.

## Why this matters for Durham buyers and lenders

Durham's office market is split sharply by submarket, which makes physical condition a pricing issue rather than a formality. In Newmark's Q3 2026 Raleigh-Durham office report, the Durham CBD posted 15.8% vacancy, while South Durham sat at 43.2% and the RTP/RDU submarket at 23.2%. Market-wide vacancy was 18.7%, and Newmark reported no office projects under construction.

Multifamily demand is steadier. Northmarq reported Raleigh-Durham apartment vacancy of 7.4% in Q3 2026, with roughly 9,400 units under construction and average cap rates in Durham only slightly above Raleigh's.

For an investor, that spread means two things. Older suburban office and flex buildings are trading at discounts, and the discount is only a bargain if you know what the roof, HVAC and parking lot will cost over the hold. Well-located multifamily and downtown assets are priced tightly, so a missed capital item comes straight out of returns. A PCA puts a number on both.

## What does a commercial PCA cover in Durham?

A commercial PCA covers the major building systems and the site, and turns what the assessor observes into a prioritized list of repairs and a forecast of capital needs. Most PCAs for lenders and institutional buyers are written to [ASTM E2018](https://store.astm.org/e2018-24.html), the industry's baseline guide for property condition assessments.

A typical scope includes:

- **Roofing:** covering, flashing, parapets, drainage and the attachment of rooftop equipment
- **Structure and envelope:** foundations, framing, masonry, exterior walls, sealants, windows, doors and balconies
- **Mechanical, electrical and plumbing:** HVAC equipment age and condition, electrical service and distribution, domestic water and sanitary systems
- **Life safety and fire safety:** fire alarm and sprinkler observations, egress, emergency lighting and fire extinguisher service tags
- **Accessibility:** a visual survey of common accessibility features
- **Site:** paving, parking, curbs, sidewalks, stormwater drainage, retaining walls and site lighting
- **Documents and interviews:** available drawings, permits, service records, prior reports and conversations with property management

The deliverable is a report with photographs, an immediate repair list and a capital reserve table for the expected hold or loan term. Agree the standard, the exclusions and the deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.

## What should a PCA look for in Durham's historic mill and warehouse buildings?

Converted tobacco and textile buildings need closer attention to masonry, timber structure, roofs and the age of systems added during conversion. Many of these conversions are now 20 to 40 years old, so the second round of capital work is arriving.

Durham's adaptive reuse history is long. The Watts and Yuille tobacco warehouses that became Brightleaf Square were built in 1904 and sold for redevelopment as ground-floor retail and upper-floor offices in 1980, according to [Open Durham](https://www.opendurham.org/buildings/brightleaf-square-watts-yuille). The American Tobacco Campus sat vacant after cigarette manufacturing there ended in 1987, and its first tenant moved into the renovated buildings in 2004, according to the [NC Department of Natural and Cultural Resources](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2016/06/24/new-american-tobacco-campus-takes-shape-2004).

### Masonry and mortar

Solid brick walls more than a century old depend on mortar condition and water management. Look for open or eroded joints, cracked or spalled brick, efflorescence, displaced lintels over openings and failed sealant at replacement windows. Repointing and masonry repair can be a significant line in the capital plan, and it needs to be scheduled before water damage reaches the interior.

### Heavy timber and floor structure

Many warehouse buildings use heavy timber columns, beams and plank floors. The assessor should note visible checking, past water staining, deflection, and any cut or notched members from later mechanical work. Concealed conditions may need a structural engineer to follow up.

### Systems installed during conversion

HVAC, electrical distribution, plumbing and fire protection in a converted building are often far younger than the shell but may be at or near the end of their own useful life. Record the installation dates from nameplates and permits, and do not assume that a historic building has historic systems, or that renovated systems are new.

### Historic designation

Properties in local historic districts or using historic tax credits may face review of exterior changes such as window replacement and masonry repair. A PCA does not determine designation or tax credit compliance. Confirm those requirements with the Durham City-County Planning Department and your counsel before you budget exterior work.

## What should a PCA look for in Durham's lab, office and flex buildings?

In office, lab and flex buildings around downtown, RTP and South Durham, the capital budget is usually driven by HVAC capacity and age, roof condition and the cost of re-tenanting vacant space.

- **Mechanical systems:** Lab and life science space requires far more outside air, exhaust and redundancy than standard office space. The PCA should record the age, capacity and condition of air handlers, rooftop units, chillers, boilers, exhaust fans and controls, and note any evidence of deferred maintenance.
- **Electrical service:** Converting office or flex space to lab use often needs more electrical capacity and emergency power. Note service size, distribution condition and generator presence and service records.
- **Roofs:** Large low-slope roofs on 1980s and 1990s suburban office and flex buildings are often on their second or third covering. Roof age, ponding, flashing and warranty status belong in the report.
- **Vacant space:** Buildings with long-vacant floors can hide inactive HVAC zones, plumbing that has not been run and water intrusion that no one has seen. Ask for access to every vacant suite.

A standard PCA reports on base building systems. It does not certify fume hoods, biosafety cabinets, specialty gases or other lab equipment. Add a specialist review if those systems are part of what you are buying.

## What should a PCA look for in Durham multifamily properties?

For Durham apartment communities, the capital budget usually turns on exterior wood components, roofs, unit HVAC and site drainage.

- **Stairs, balconies and breezeways:** Wood stairs and balconies on 1980s and 1990s garden communities deteriorate under Piedmont humidity and heavy summer rain. Look for rot, loose railings, corroded connectors and failed flashing at ledger boards.
- **Siding and trim:** Note damaged or deteriorated siding, trim and sealant, and evidence of water behind the cladding.
- **Roofs:** Record roof age, covering type and any evidence of leaks or patching on each building.
- **Unit HVAC and water heaters:** Sample units and record equipment ages, since staggered replacement is often the largest recurring reserve item.
- **Site drainage:** Durham's red clay soils drain slowly. Look for erosion, ponding, settled walks and pavement failure near downspouts and inlets.

## How do lenders use a PCA on Durham deals?

Lenders use the PCA to size required repairs and capital reserves and to confirm that the collateral is in the condition the borrower describes. Many commercial lenders, along with agency, CMBS and SBA programs, set their own report requirements on top of ASTM E2018, such as a specific reserve term, cost tables or a particular report format.

Get the lender's written requirements before you request a proposal. A PCA that does not match the lender's template can delay closing while the report is reissued. ## How do you order a PCA in Durham?

Ordering a PCA goes fastest when you send complete information up front.

1. **Define the decision.** State whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or internal capital planning.
2. **Collect lender requirements.** Get the lender's written report requirements and reserve term.
3. **Send property details.** Provide the address, property type, building size, year built and any conversion or renovation dates, the number of buildings, known issues and your deadline.
4. **Share documents.** Send available drawings, permits, roof warranties, service contracts and prior reports. Permit history can be requested from [Durham City-County Building and Safety](https://www.durhamnc.gov/293/Permits-Inspections).
5. **Arrange access.** Ask the seller or property manager to confirm roof, mechanical room, vacant suite and tenant access before the site visit.
6. **Review the draft.** Trace every major finding to its photographs and assign an owner to each open item before closing.

## What does a PCA not replace?

A PCA reports physical conditions that can be observed during the site visit. It does not replace a Phase I environmental site assessment, a zoning report, title review, lease review, a survey, a historic designation review or legal advice. It is not a code compliance inspection. Concealed conditions and systems that were not tested may need follow-up by a structural engineer, roofer, mechanical contractor or other specialist.
