A property condition assessment (PCA) in Norfolk or Virginia Beach is a walk-through survey and document review of a commercial property that identifies physical deficiencies, immediate repairs and expected capital needs. In Hampton Roads, the assessment has to take water seriously: tidal and storm flooding, ground-floor and below-grade equipment, salt-air corrosion and roof attachment drive more capital surprises here than almost anywhere else in Virginia.

C&J Property Assessments performs PCAs, pre-contract inspections and capital needs assessments across Hampton Roads for commercial real estate investors, lenders and private equity funds.

## Why this matters for Hampton Roads buyers and lenders

Hampton Roads is a large, trade- and defense-driven market where condition risk and flood risk are closely linked. Cushman & Wakefield | Thalhimer counted about 120.7 million square feet of industrial inventory in the region in Q2 2026, with vacancy at 8.0% and about 3.5 million square feet under construction. CBRE's Q3 2026 Norfolk industrial figures put vacancy at 7.7% after 605,000 square feet of positive net absorption in the quarter. On the multifamily side, Northmarq reported Q2 2026 vacancy of 4.6% and year-over-year asking rent growth of 5.4%, with roughly 2,000 units under construction.

That mix means investors are underwriting everything from new port-adjacent warehouses to 1970s and 1980s garden apartments and older downtown Norfolk office buildings. The building ages differ, but the question is the same: what will this property cost you after closing? On a low-lying parcel, a building that shows well on tour can hide corroded electrical gear, a patched roof from past storms or a parking lot that floods on a king tide. Those items become your capital budget the day you take title.

## What does a commercial PCA cover in Norfolk and Virginia Beach?

A commercial PCA covers the major building systems and the site, and turns what the assessor observes into a prioritized list of repairs and a forecast of capital needs. Most PCAs in the region are written to [ASTM E2018](https://store.astm.org/e2018-24.html), the industry's baseline guide for property condition assessments.

A typical scope includes:

- **Roofing:** covering, flashing, edge metal, drainage and the attachment of rooftop equipment
- **Structure and envelope:** foundations, framing, exterior walls, sealants, windows, doors and balconies
- **Mechanical, electrical and plumbing:** HVAC equipment age and condition, electrical service and distribution, domestic water, sanitary and any sump or ejector pumps
- **Life safety and fire safety:** fire alarm and sprinkler observations, egress, emergency lighting and fire extinguisher service tags
- **Accessibility:** a visual survey of common accessibility features
- **Site:** paving, parking, curbs, sidewalks, stormwater drainage, bulkheads and retaining walls, and site lighting
- **Documents and interviews:** available drawings, permits, service records, prior reports, flood history and conversations with property management

The deliverable is a report with photographs, an immediate repair list and a capital reserve table for the expected hold or loan term. Agree the standard, the exclusions and the deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.

## Which flood and coastal conditions should a Hampton Roads PCA look for?

Flooding is the single biggest difference between a Hampton Roads PCA and one in central Virginia or the Carolina Piedmont. Ask your assessor to address these conditions specifically.

### Recurrent tidal and rainfall flooding

Relative sea-level rise is a measured, ongoing problem in Norfolk. According to a [Pew Charitable Trusts brief on Norfolk's zoning](https://www.pewtrusts.org/research-and-analysis/issue-briefs/2019/11/norfolks-revised-zoning-ordinance-aims-to-improve-flood-resilience), sea level there has risen more than a foot in the last 80 years, with land subsidence accounting for about half of that rise. The result is "nuisance" flooding on high tides and heavy rain, not only during hurricanes.

A useful PCA looks for the physical evidence: high-water lines and staining on walls and doors, corrosion at the base of storefront frames, damaged or replaced lower drywall, efflorescence on masonry, and sediment in catch basins and storm inlets. Ask the property manager directly how often the parking lot, loading docks or ground floor have taken water, and record the answer.

### Equipment elevation

Where a building's electrical switchgear, meters, transformers, boilers, elevator machinery or fire pump sit relative to the floor and the surrounding grade matters a great deal in Hampton Roads. Equipment in basements, at-grade mechanical rooms and ground-level electrical rooms is exposed every time water gets in. The assessor should record where critical equipment sits, any evidence that it has been flooded or replaced, and whether sump and ejector pumps are present and serviced.

### Salt air and corrosion

Properties near the Chesapeake Bay, the Elizabeth River and the Virginia Beach oceanfront see faster corrosion on exposed metal. Typical findings include rusted rooftop HVAC cabinets and condenser coils, failing roof edge metal and fasteners, corroded exterior stairs, railings and balcony connections, and deteriorated light poles. Corrosion shortens useful life, so it changes the capital reserve schedule as well as the repair list.

### Wind and roof attachment

Hampton Roads sits in the Atlantic hurricane track, and nor'easters bring sustained wind and surge outside hurricane season. Ask the assessor to look at roof covering condition, perimeter securement, flashing and the tie-downs on rooftop units, and to note evidence of past storm repairs, roof age and available warranty information.

### Flood zone status

A PCA does not determine flood zone status. Confirm the designation through the [FEMA Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal) and your lender's requirements, and treat flood insurance and elevation certificates as a separate workstream.

## How do Norfolk's resilience rules affect a capital plan?

Norfolk's zoning ordinance includes resilience requirements that can affect redevelopment and major renovation, so they belong on the due diligence checklist for any value-add plan. The revised ordinance took effect in 2018. It created a Coastal Resilience Overlay with elevation requirements for flood-prone areas and a "resilience quotient" point system that new development and redevelopment must meet, scoring flood risk reduction, stormwater management and energy resilience.

Separately, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the city are building a coastal storm risk management project with floodwalls and pump stations. [WHRO reported in March 2026](https://www.whro.org/environment/2026-03-23/cost-of-norfolk-floodwall-project-balloons-to-6-billion-completion-slated-for-2037) that the Corps now estimates the project at about $6.1 billion with completion slated for 2037, and that it is designed for major storms with limited effect on tidal flooding.

A PCA does not interpret zoning or forecast public infrastructure. What it does is document the building's physical condition so you and your counsel can judge what a renovation or repositioning plan will trigger. Confirm overlay and permitting requirements with the City of Norfolk or the City of Virginia Beach before you finalize a capital budget.

## Which property types need a PCA in Hampton Roads?

Any commercial asset being bought, refinanced or held for capital planning can justify a PCA. In Hampton Roads, these property types come up most often.

- **Industrial and logistics:** Warehouses and distribution buildings serving the Port of Virginia in Norfolk, Portsmouth, Chesapeake and Suffolk. Newer buildings need a baseline PCA documenting warranties, punch-list items and early drainage problems. Older stock needs closer attention to roofs, dock equipment, slab condition and paving.
- **Multifamily:** Garden-style communities across Virginia Beach, Chesapeake and Norfolk, much of it occupied by military households. Exterior stairs, balconies, breezeways, siding, roof age and unit HVAC usually drive the capital budget.
- **Office:** Downtown Norfolk and Virginia Beach Town Center office buildings, including older stock being considered for conversion. Conversions raise questions about mechanical capacity, plumbing, elevators and accessibility.
- **Retail:** Strip centers and neighborhood retail along corridors such as Virginia Beach Boulevard and Military Highway, where roofs, parking lots and rooftop HVAC dominate the reserve schedule.
- **Hospitality:** Oceanfront hotels in Virginia Beach face heavy wear on HVAC equipment, finishes, balconies and exterior components from salt exposure and seasonal occupancy.

## How do lenders use a PCA on Hampton Roads deals?

Lenders use the PCA to size required repairs and capital reserves and to confirm that the collateral is in the condition the borrower describes. Many commercial lenders, along with agency, CMBS and SBA programs, set their own report requirements on top of ASTM E2018, such as a specific reserve term, cost tables or a particular report format.

Get the lender's written requirements before you request a proposal. A PCA that does not match the lender's template can delay closing while the report is reissued. ## How do you order a PCA in Norfolk or Virginia Beach?

Ordering a PCA goes fastest when you send complete information up front.

1. **Define the decision.** State whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or internal capital planning.
2. **Collect lender requirements.** Get the lender's written report requirements and reserve term.
3. **Send property details.** Provide the address, property type, building size, year built, number of buildings, known issues, any flood history and your deadline.
4. **Share documents.** Send available drawings, permits, roof warranties, service contracts, prior reports and any elevation certificate.
5. **Arrange access.** Ask the seller or property manager to confirm roof, equipment room, basement and tenant access before the site visit.
6. **Review the draft.** Trace every major finding to its photographs and assign an owner to each open item before closing.

## What does a PCA not replace?

A PCA reports physical conditions that can be observed during the site visit. It does not replace a Phase I environmental site assessment, a zoning report, title review, lease review, a survey, a flood determination or legal advice. It is not a code compliance inspection. Concealed conditions and systems that were not tested may need follow-up by a structural engineer, roofer, electrician or other specialist.
