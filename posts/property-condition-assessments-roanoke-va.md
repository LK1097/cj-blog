A property condition assessment (PCA) in Roanoke, VA is a walk-through survey and document review of a commercial property that identifies physical deficiencies, immediate repairs and expected capital needs. In Roanoke, the questions that most often move the numbers are flood history along the Roanoke River and its tributary creeks, the condition of older masonry buildings downtown and in the historic neighborhoods, and the age of roofs, HVAC and paving on the valley's medical office, retail and industrial properties.

## Why this matters for Roanoke buyers and lenders

Roanoke is the commercial center of western Virginia, and much of its income property is older than the buyers underwriting it. The city grew as a railroad town, and its downtown and surrounding districts hold a large stock of early twentieth-century masonry offices, warehouses and commercial buildings. Many of those buildings have been converted to apartments, offices and mixed uses over the last two decades, often with historic rehabilitation tax credits administered by the [Virginia Department of Historic Resources](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/programs/tax-credits/). A conversion can look new inside while the roof, masonry, foundations and original structure carry decades of wear.

Health care is the other anchor. Carilion Clinic is headquartered in Roanoke, and medical and professional office buildings cluster around its main campus and along the city's commercial corridors. Retail centers, flex and light industrial buildings and garden apartments fill out the rest of the investment market in the city, Salem and Roanoke County.

Then there is the river. On November 4, 1985, the Roanoke River at Roanoke reached a record crest of 23.35 feet against a flood stage of 10 feet, according to the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/media/rnk/past_events/Flood%20of%201985_Then-Now_2020.pdf). The NWS counts at least ten deaths in the Roanoke Valley and estimates damage that may have approached $225 million there. Buildings that took water in 1985, or in later storms, can still carry repairs and hidden damage that a buyer needs to understand before closing.

## What does a commercial PCA cover in Roanoke?

A commercial PCA covers the major building systems and the site, and turns what the assessor observes into a prioritized list of repairs and a forecast of capital needs. Most PCAs for lenders and institutional buyers are written to [ASTM E2018](https://store.astm.org/e2018-24.html), the industry's baseline guide for property condition assessments.

A typical scope includes:

- **Roofing:** covering, flashing, parapets, drainage and the attachment of rooftop equipment
- **Structure and envelope:** foundations, framing, masonry, exterior walls, sealants, windows, doors and balconies
- **Mechanical, electrical and plumbing:** HVAC equipment age and condition, electrical service and distribution, domestic water and sanitary systems
- **Life safety and fire safety:** fire alarm and sprinkler observations, egress, emergency lighting and fire extinguisher service tags
- **Accessibility:** a visual survey of common accessibility features
- **Site:** paving, parking, curbs, sidewalks, stormwater drainage, retaining walls and site lighting
- **Documents and interviews:** available drawings, permits, service records, prior reports and conversations with property management

The deliverable is a report with photographs, an immediate repair list and a capital reserve table for the expected hold or loan term. Agree the standard, the exclusions and the deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.

## How should a PCA handle flood risk in Roanoke?

A PCA should record visible evidence of past flooding and the features that limit future water damage, then point the buyer to the separate reports that answer the regulatory questions. The condition survey and the flood zone determination are different deliverables, and lenders usually require both.

### What the flood reduction project does and does not do

After 1985, the city and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built the Roanoke River Flood Reduction Project, a $72 million effort that bought flood-prone properties and built levees, flood walls and flood storage benches along the river. [Cardinal News reports](https://cardinalnews.org/2023/11/03/thirty-eight-years-after-the-flood-of-85-roanoke-is-still-preparing-for-the-next-one/) that the project concluded in 2012 and was built to contain a 25-year flood and to mitigate, not prevent, a 100-year flood. The same article notes that the city is still addressing flooding on some of the 13 tributaries inside city limits. In 1985, the NWS also recorded flooding on Tinker Creek, Mason Creek and Peters Creek. A property away from the river is not automatically a property without water risk.

### Signs of past flood damage

Look for water lines on walls and equipment, replaced drywall or flooring at a consistent height, rusted door frames and electrical boxes near the floor, efflorescence on lower masonry, and mismatched materials that suggest a partial rebuild. Ask the seller and property manager directly about past flood events and insurance claims, and request the related repair records.

### Equipment elevation and basements

Many older Roanoke buildings have basements that hold boilers, electrical service, switchgear and water heaters. Note where that equipment sits relative to grade and to the nearest creek. Equipment in a basement near a waterway is exposed to the next flood, and relocating it can be a large capital item.

### Hillside drainage

The Roanoke Valley is ringed by mountains, and many sites sit on slopes. Record water running toward buildings, failed downspout connections, ponding on paving, clogged inlets and leaning or cracked retaining walls. Steep sites can collect heavy runoff during short storms even when the river stays in its banks.

### What the PCA does not decide

A PCA does not determine a property's flood zone, its base flood elevation or whether past repairs met floodplain rules. Get a flood zone determination through your lender, check FEMA flood maps, and confirm floodplain development requirements with the local jurisdiction before you budget elevation or floodproofing work.

## What should a PCA look for in Roanoke's historic and converted buildings?

In older masonry buildings and conversions, the capital budget usually turns on roofs, masonry, windows, structure and the quality of the conversion work. The goal is to separate what was truly replaced during a renovation from what was only covered up.

- **Masonry:** Look for open mortar joints, cracked or spalled brick, displaced lintels, failing parapets and efflorescence. Repointing and parapet repair on a multistory building is a significant line item.
- **Roofs:** Older buildings often have low-slope roofs with multiple layers, internal drains and many penetrations. Record roof type, age, ponding, patching and the condition of flashing at parapets and rooftop equipment.
- **Windows:** Historic projects sometimes repair original windows instead of replacing them. Note rot, failed glazing putty, broken sash cords and air and water leaks.
- **Structure:** Look for cracking, sagging floors, water-damaged framing and alterations made during the conversion, such as new openings in bearing walls. Concealed structural questions may need a structural engineer.
- **Conversion systems:** Record the age and type of the HVAC, electrical and plumbing installed during the conversion, and ask for permits and closeout documents. Systems installed during a tax-credit rehabilitation often reach replacement age together.
- **Elevators:** Note the elevator type, the date of the last modernization and the current inspection certificate.
- **Life safety:** Check fire alarm and sprinkler observations, exit routes, emergency lighting and fire separations where visible. Older buildings converted to residential use carry higher occupancy loads than they were built for.

If a historic tax credit is part of the deal, keep in mind that future exterior work may need to meet historic preservation standards. Budget for that in the reserve schedule rather than assuming the cheapest replacement.

## What should a PCA look for in Roanoke medical office, retail and industrial buildings?

For medical office, the capital budget usually turns on HVAC capacity, plumbing, roofs and accessibility. For retail and industrial, it turns on roofs, paving and building envelopes.

- **Medical office HVAC:** Exam rooms, imaging suites and procedure spaces carry heavier mechanical loads than standard offices. Record rooftop unit and air handler ages, and note supplemental cooling added by tenants.
- **Accessibility:** Patients include people with limited mobility, so record visible accessibility features at parking, entrances, restrooms and routes between buildings. A PCA survey of accessibility is not a full accessibility compliance audit.
- **Retail roofs and paving:** Strip centers in Roanoke and Roanoke County often have large low-slope roofs and parking lots near the end of their useful life. Record roof type, age, ponding and patching, and note cracking, potholes and drainage failures in the lot.
- **Industrial and flex buildings:** Record roof and wall panel condition, dock doors and levelers, slab cracking, truck court paving and any visible evidence of past process or storage use. Older industrial buildings in the valley may have been altered many times.
- **Freeze-thaw wear:** Western Virginia sees more freeze-thaw cycles than coastal markets. Note cracked paving, heaved sidewalks, spalled concrete and damaged sealants that winter weather makes worse.

Tenant-specific medical equipment, such as imaging machines or medical gas systems, is outside a standard PCA. Add a specialist review if those systems matter to your underwriting.

## How do lenders use a PCA on Roanoke deals?

Lenders use the PCA to size required repairs and capital reserves and to confirm that the collateral is in the condition the borrower describes. Many commercial lenders, along with agency, CMBS and SBA programs, set their own report requirements on top of ASTM E2018, such as a specific reserve term, cost tables or a particular report format.

Get the lender's written requirements before you request a proposal. A PCA that does not match the lender's template can delay closing while the report is reissued. ## How do you order a PCA in Roanoke?

Ordering a PCA goes fastest when you send complete information up front.

1. **Define the decision.** State whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or internal capital planning.
2. **Collect lender requirements.** Get the lender's written report requirements and reserve term.
3. **Send property details.** Provide the address, property type, building size, year built, any conversion or renovation dates, unit count, the number of buildings, known issues, any flood history and your deadline.
4. **Share documents.** Send available drawings, permits, roof warranties, service contracts, elevator certificates, flood repair records and prior reports. For historic tax credit projects, include the approved rehabilitation scope if you have it.
5. **Arrange access.** Ask the seller or property manager to confirm roof, basement, mechanical room, vacant unit and occupied unit access before the site visit.
6. **Review the draft.** Trace every major finding to its photographs and assign an owner to each open item before closing.

## What does a PCA not replace?

A PCA reports physical conditions that can be observed during the site visit. It does not replace a Phase I environmental site assessment, a flood zone determination, a zoning report, title review, lease review, a survey or legal advice. It is not a code compliance inspection. Virginia buildings are regulated under the [Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code](https://dhcd.virginia.gov/virginia-uniform-statewide-building-code-usbc), which local building departments enforce. Confirm permit history and open code matters with the City of Roanoke, Roanoke County or the City of Salem, depending on where the property sits. The City of Roanoke offers online permit search through its [eTRAKiT portal](https://trakit.roanokeva.gov/etrakit). Concealed conditions and systems that were not tested may need follow-up by a structural engineer, roofer, mechanical contractor or other specialist.
