A property condition assessment (PCA) in Winston-Salem, NC is a walk-through survey and document review of a commercial property that identifies physical deficiencies, immediate repairs and expected capital needs. ## What does a commercial PCA cover in Winston-Salem?

A commercial PCA covers the major building systems and the site, and turns what the assessor observes into a prioritized list of repairs and a forecast of capital needs. Most PCAs for lenders and institutional buyers are written to [ASTM E2018](https://store.astm.org/e2018-24.html), the industry's baseline guide for property condition assessments.

A typical scope includes:

- **Roofing:** covering, flashing, parapets, drainage and the attachment of rooftop equipment
- **Structure and envelope:** foundations, framing, masonry, exterior walls, sealants, windows, doors and balconies
- **Mechanical, electrical and plumbing:** HVAC equipment age and condition, electrical service and distribution, domestic water and sanitary systems
- **Life safety and fire safety:** fire alarm and sprinkler observations, egress, emergency lighting and fire extinguisher service tags
- **Accessibility:** a visual survey of common accessibility features
- **Site:** paving, parking, curbs, sidewalks, stormwater drainage, retaining walls and site lighting
- **Documents and interviews:** available drawings, permits, service records, prior reports and conversations with property management

The deliverable is a report with photographs, an immediate repair list and a capital reserve table for the expected hold or loan term. Agree the standard, the exclusions and the deliverables in writing before fieldwork starts.

## What should a PCA look for in converted tobacco and textile buildings?

Converted industrial buildings need closer attention to masonry, structure, roofs and the age of the systems added during the conversion. The shell may be a century old while the HVAC is 15 years old, and both timelines belong in the capital plan.

### Masonry and water management

Multi-story brick factories depend on sound mortar, lintels and parapets to keep water out. Look for open or eroded joints, cracked or spalled brick, efflorescence, rusting steel lintels, displaced parapet coping and failed sealant around replacement windows. Repointing and parapet repair can be large line items, and deferring them lets water reach interior finishes and structure.

### Structure and floor loading

Many tobacco buildings were built with heavy timber or early concrete frames sized for warehouse loads. The assessor should note visible cracking, spalling or exposed reinforcing in concrete, checking or water staining in timber, and any members cut or notched for later mechanical work. Concealed conditions may need a structural engineer to follow up.

### Conversion-era systems

HVAC, electrical distribution, elevators, plumbing and fire protection installed during conversion are often 10 to 25 years old. Record installation dates from nameplates, permits and service records, and plan for staggered replacement rather than assuming a renovated building is new.

### Lab and research space

Research buildings in the Innovation Quarter carry heavier mechanical and electrical loads than standard office space. A standard PCA reports on base building systems, such as air handlers, exhaust fans, chillers, boilers and emergency power, but does not certify lab equipment like fume hoods, biosafety cabinets or specialty gas systems. Add a specialist review if those systems are part of the underwriting.

### Historic designation

Properties in historic districts or using historic tax credits may face review of exterior changes such as window replacement and masonry repair. A PCA does not determine designation or tax credit compliance. Confirm those requirements with the City-County Planning staff and your counsel before you budget exterior work.

## What should a PCA look for in Winston-Salem industrial buildings?

In industrial and flex buildings, the capital budget usually turns on the roof, the slab, the dock equipment and the paving.

- **Roofs:** Large low-slope roofs on older warehouses and former manufacturing plants are often on their second or third covering. Record roof type, age, ponding, flashing condition and warranty status.
- **Slabs:** Note cracking, joint deterioration, settlement and any evidence of heavy point loads from former manufacturing use.
- **Dock equipment:** Count and assess dock doors, levelers, seals and bumpers, which wear quickly under heavy truck traffic.
- **Paving and truck courts:** Asphalt and concrete truck courts fail at drainage points and turning areas. Note cracking, rutting and ponding.
- **Fire protection:** Record sprinkler system type and visible condition, fire pump presence and service tags. Changes in storage use can raise questions about sprinkler design that a fire protection engineer should confirm.

## What should a PCA look for in Winston-Salem multifamily properties?

For apartment communities, the capital budget usually turns on exterior wood components, roofs, unit HVAC and site drainage.

- **Stairs, balconies and breezeways:** Wood stairs and balconies on 1970s through 1990s garden communities deteriorate under Piedmont humidity and summer storms. Look for rot, loose railings, corroded connectors and failed flashing at ledger boards.
- **Siding and trim:** Note damaged siding, trim and sealant, and evidence of water behind the cladding.
- **Roofs:** Record roof age, covering type and evidence of leaks or patching on each building.
- **Unit HVAC and water heaters:** Sample units and record equipment ages, since staggered replacement is often the largest recurring reserve item.
- **Site drainage:** The region's clay soils drain slowly. Look for erosion, ponding, settled walks and pavement failure near downspouts and inlets.

## How do lenders use a PCA on Winston-Salem deals?

Lenders use the PCA to size required repairs and capital reserves and to confirm that the collateral is in the condition the borrower describes. Many commercial lenders, along with agency, CMBS and SBA programs, set their own report requirements on top of ASTM E2018, such as a specific reserve term, cost tables or a particular report format.

Get the lender's written requirements before you request a proposal. A PCA that does not match the lender's template can delay closing while the report is reissued. ## How do you order a PCA in Winston-Salem?

Ordering a PCA goes fastest when you send complete information up front.

1. **Define the decision.** State whether the report supports an acquisition, a refinance, a lender requirement or internal capital planning.
2. **Collect lender requirements.** Get the lender's written report requirements and reserve term.
3. **Send property details.** Provide the address, property type, building size, year built and any conversion dates, the number of buildings, known issues and your deadline.
4. **Share documents.** Send available drawings, permits, roof warranties, service contracts and prior reports.
5. **Arrange access.** Ask the seller or property manager to confirm roof, mechanical room, vacant suite and tenant access before the site visit.
6. **Review the draft.** Trace every major finding to its photographs and assign an owner to each open item before closing.

## What does a PCA not replace?

A PCA reports physical conditions that can be observed during the site visit. It does not replace a Phase I environmental site assessment, a zoning report, title review, lease review, a survey, a historic designation review or legal advice. It is not a code compliance inspection. Concealed conditions and systems that were not tested may need follow-up by a structural engineer, roofer, mechanical contractor or other specialist.
